**Tier 7 · Depends on: F056 (missions), F070 (the orchestrator loop inside Remedy), F300 (the structure measure), F271 (replacing as deleting) · Blocks/used by: every mission of more than a few jobs on any project; Luna's second transition, in which Remedy does the normal code work of the universe workspace; F248**

## Goal & Done
A mission Remedy runs on any project keeps that project clean by itself. After every fifth
completed job of a mission, the next job is an upkeep job that Remedy plans from its own records:
the findings earlier jobs left open, the project's structure measured against the project's own
ledger, and what was replaced and not yet deleted. The operator who lets Remedy build a project
can rely on this without asking for it. DONE when a fixture mission of six jobs gets its sixth as
an upkeep job that names a planted open finding and a planted oversized file, and nothing of the
mission, job and task model changed shape for it.

## Why this exists
Two projects of the operator were built by agents over weeks: Remedy itself and the universe
workspace. What kept them workable was never one good session; it was a process around the
sessions: a plan worked in order, findings with owners paid down on a cadence, a ledger of
structural debt, replacing as deleting, and a person's approval. Where a piece was missing, the
damage was measured: in Remedy a function of 1,522 lines grew for want of a ratchet, and in the
universe workspace every structural debtor grew on the day its ledger began. Today that process
lives in the build loop's protocol documents and in a skill of the coding agent that builds
Remedy, outside the product. A mission's jobs carry a reviewer's findings per run
(`final_job_review.json` and the rounds of a run) and nothing collects what stayed open across
jobs, nothing measures the project's structure, and nothing schedules cleanup. A model that writes
excellent code does not by itself keep a codebase of a quarter of a million lines coherent over
weeks; that is memory and enforcement across sessions, and it is what Remedy is for.

## Task slicing
- T001 — Inventory. Where findings live in a job's records today, what "still open after its job"
  means for each kind, and what the orchestrator loop and `remedy mission continue` know about a
  mission's finished jobs. Written to an inventory file under `.agent/`. No behaviour changes.
- T002 — The project's upkeep ledger. Records under the project in the data root, written by the
  jobs' own closing steps: the findings a job left open with the job that raised them, the
  structure measures of F300's command with the project's recorded limits, and what a job replaced
  and did not delete.
- T003 — The cadence. The orchestrator loop and `remedy mission continue` add an upkeep job after
  every fifth completed job of a mission; the number is a setting with the default five, and the
  mission's plan says when the next one is due. Skipping one is a decision that is recorded, never
  a silence. The upkeep job's plan is compiled from the ledger by fixed rules — the oldest open
  findings first, the worst structure measure, every replaced and not deleted item — inside the
  mission's own budgets. It is an ordinary job with ordinary evidence and the ordinary approval.
- T004 — Replacing is deleting, inside a job. A task whose plan says it replaces something names
  what it deletes; the reviewer fails a round that leaves the old beside the new; what must wait
  enters the ledger and the next upkeep job.
- T005 — Visible. `remedy mission show`, the digest and the public API say how many jobs remain
  until the next upkeep job and what it will carry.

## Acceptance
- In the fixture mission the sixth job is an upkeep job whose plan names the planted open finding
  and the planted oversized file; a planted replacement without its deletion is in the ledger and
  in that plan.
- Skipping an upkeep job without a recorded decision is impossible through the command line.
- A mission record and a job record written before this feature load and run unchanged.
- The digest names the jobs left until the next upkeep job.
- R-1233 is resolved: a test dispatches, through the loop, a job whose plan gate is open
  and finds it approved, audited, before it ran.
- R-1234 is resolved: a job halted blocked or stopped gets its line in the upkeep ledger once a
  later job of its mission is linked, held by a test.

## Do not touch
The mission, job and task model as it stands: this feature adds a ledger, a rule in the loop and a
job like any other. The approval gate. No clock: the cadence counts jobs, never time.

## Orchestrator brief
T001 first. Prefer rules a person can read in one paragraph over a model's judgment: which
findings count, which measure is worst, what the upkeep job may spend.

  <reason>` is the one way to skip it, and the reason is recorded; the orchestrator never skips.

  one it replaces, the halted job's line records the pair, and an upkeep plan carries it while both
  files are in the repository; held through a real run.
- T005: `remedy mission show` says when the next upkeep job comes and what it would carry, and the
  client digest, which the public API's digest answers, gives each mission `upkeep_every`,
  `upkeep_jobs_left` and `upkeep_open_findings`; the client interface is at 1.6. Neither writes.
