## Goal & Done
A finished run becomes a story you can hand to someone: chapters
derive from phases plus key events (decisions, failures, fixes),
autoplay scrubs the timeline with narration cards from the humanize
catalog, and export produces ONE self-contained HTML file — inline
data and assets — that plays anywhere without Remedy. DONE when the
demo recording plays as a coherent chaptered story, narration cards
match goldens (catalog-sourced, no invented drama), the export
opens from file:// in a clean browser with zero network requests,
and export size stays within a documented budget.

## How it fits (inspect current shape before building)
Everything narrative already exists: the phase mapping and scrubber
(replay IS scrubbing with direction and pacing), the humanize
catalog (narration lines = the same vocabulary, composed into
cards), key-event extraction (the timeline's sub-glyph set —
decisions, failures, heals, stops — chapters break on phase
boundaries and cluster key events within). The export is the novel
part: a build step bundling the reducer, renderer subset, event
data and assets into one HTML — scope it honestly (the story
player subset, not the whole cockpit; interactions limited to
play/pause/scrub/chapter-jump).

## Design (suggested shape)
- Chaptering: pure over the ledger — one chapter per phase with
  events; phases dense in key events split at a documented
  threshold; chapter titles from a small template set (the
  planning chapter is "The plan", not LLM poetry — deterministic,
  golden).
- Narration cards: at each key event, a card composes the humanize
  line + the relevant metric (cost so far, verdict) + the actor
  where ownership knows one; cards render per the reference's
  narration treatment synced to the scrub position.
- Autoplay: paced scrubbing (config speed, chapter pauses) on the
  timeline's machinery; the SCRUBBED/LIVE distinction inherits
  (stories of running jobs play the recorded prefix and say so).
- Export: `remedy job story <id> --export story.html` — inline
  JSON data (envelope subset), the player bundle, fonts subset per
  assets_spec licensing (verify embeddability there — a finding,
  not an assumption), a size budget (config, single-digit MB) with
  large-evidence linking rules like the certificate's.
- The certificate can embed/link the story (cross-ref, later
  wiring noted there).

## Task slicing
- **T001** chaptering + key-event clustering + title templates +
  goldens on fixture ledgers.
- **T002** narration cards + sync + autoplay pacing + the in-app
  story mode on the demo recording (golden walkthrough).
- **T003** the export build (bundling, inline assets, the font

  (file://, network monitor zero) + docs.

## Acceptance
- Demo story chapters/narration golden. Autoplay pacing and
  chapter pauses per config; scrub/keyboard work in-app and in
  export. Export: zero network, plays in the test browser matrix,
  within budget on the fixture; oversized evidence linked not
  inlined. No narration text outside catalog+template sources
  (the no-drama grep).

## Edge cases & assumption defaults (A9)
- Ugly runs export honestly (a failure-heavy story is the trust
  artifact — no green-washing vocabulary, the certificate's rule).
  Ancient format versions: the exporter refuses mismatched
  envelope versions with the honest message rather than guessing.

## Built State (F039, 2026-09-29)

What exists on disk at the close of F039, built in ten rounds over two sessions on
`feature/f039-story-replay-mode`, cut from `main` at `4d60eb84`. The story reads a job's own record
and changes nothing; every sentence it shows comes from the humanize catalog or one of its own small
tables, never from a model. Every DECISION named lives in `.agent/decisions.md`; every round's
verdict in `.agent/live_review.md` or, once rotated, its archive.

**T001 — the chapters.** `apps/ui/src/components/story/storyChapters.ts` is a pure fold over the
ledger rows the phase bar reads (DECISION F039 D1). A chapter is a phase with a non-zero span in
`readPhases` over the whole ledger, so the chapters tile the ledger and a skipped phase gives none;
the key events are `extractSubGlyphs`' decisions, failures, heals and stops, clustered when each
comes at most two seqs after the one before; a phase with more than six key events splits into
parts of six. The titles are one table, The start, The plan, The build, The tests, The review and
The finish, a split part reading `<title>, part <n> of <parts>`; a title names the phase and never
its outcome, and The finish exists only where every task passed.
`tests/ui_contracts/test_story_chapters.py` pins the table to the bar's phases and forbids any other
sentence in the module.

**T002 — the narration, the pacing and the in-app story.** `storyNarration.ts` (DECISION F039 D3)
makes one card per cluster: each key event a beat with the catalog's line, the reviewer's verdict for
a review round from `STORY_VERDICT_LINES`, and the ownership ledger's own sentence as its actor when
exactly one entry and one event agree; the card carries the cost so far through `costMetricOf`, and
`chapterAt` and `cardAt` place a scrub position. `storyAutoplay.ts` (D4) answers the next position and
the wait, one ledger event per step with a chapter pause before each new chapter, chapter to chapter
under reduced motion, its defaults the motion reference's node birth and pulse. The keys
`story.step_ms` and `story.chapter_pause_ms` (420 and 1600) reach the browser as the dashboard's
`story` section, a budget tick's figures ride on its feed row, and `storyView.ts` assembles the view
(D5). `storyPlayer.ts` holds the position in words, where Play starts, the reached beats, the
running-job line and `storyWalk`, the golden walkthrough the demo recording is tested against;
`StoryPanel.tsx` is the docked, portaled card opened by the Story button of `RightLivePanel.tsx`,
driving the timeline's own scrub with one timer that re-arms only on play, position and reduced motion
(D6, and R-1101's repair). R-1102's repair keeps a task's own id in `normalizeDashboardPayload`, so a
finished `remedy do` job reaches Finalized on the phase bar and its story reaches The finish.

**T003 — the export.** `packages/orchestration/story_export.py` builds a job's story payload,
`remedy.story.v1`, from the cockpit's own builders: the dashboard's task, live and story sections,
every event through `_safe_event_summary`, and `ownership_view` (D7); `storyExport.ts` reads it back
through the cockpit's own decoders and refuses another schema by name. `apps/ui/vite.config.ts`'s
plugin builds the player a second time, after the cockpit, as one script and one style sheet in the
`story` subdirectory of the resolved `outDir` (D8, D9); `storyPlayerMain.tsx` reads the story from
the page's `remedy-story-data` element and mounts `StoryPlayerApp`, the phase bar over the story
panel with no Close button. `remedy job story <id> --export <file>` writes one page inlining the
player and the payload under a content security policy of `default-src 'none'`, every `<` in the JSON
written `\u003c`; it refuses a player not built (exit 3) and a file over `story.export_max_bytes`,
5,000,000 bytes by default (exit 1), and never cuts a story. No font is bundled: the asset authority
forbids fonts written into CSS and the cockpit installs none yet, so the page uses the cockpit's own
font stacks (the assumption log's F039 row). `tests/ui_server/test_story_export_file_live.py` is the
acceptance spine (D9): it runs the demo job on the fake providers, exports its story around a player
built into a temporary folder, and drives headless Chrome over `--remote-debugging-pipe`, requiring
the demo's three chapters, the slider's arrow key, a chapter jump, Space for Play and Pause, no
request but the file itself, and no exception or console error while it plays and for two seconds
after (R-1103). `docs/guides/story-user-guide-v1.md` is the operator's guide.

**Not built.** The certificate this feature file cross-references does not exist, so nothing embeds
or links a story yet. The export carries no evidence file, so the feature file's rule for linking
oversized evidence has nothing to apply to. The test browser is headless Chrome alone (D9).
