## Goal & Done
A client can ask Remedy over HTTP what runs right now, look into one job's worker, and learn why
it hangs and what the operator can do, all in closed words. DONE when:
- **`GET /api/v1/now`** (twin `remedy now --json`) answers every living job and every waiting
  order with `job`, `order`, `project`, `state`, `phase` (`plan`, `build`, `review`, `repair`,
  `apply_wait`), `task` (id and title), `round` (n of `max_rounds`), `worker` (provider and model
  of the role that works now), `budget` (`used` and `limits` for tokens, calls, minutes and
  `waited_s`), `last_output_s` (seconds since the worker's last output), `stalled` (true from a
  threshold in configuration, default 300 seconds without output while the process lives) with
  `stall_reason` in closed words (`waiting_decision`, `waiting_lock`, `model_unreachable`,
  `model_slow`, `no_output`, `none`), and `waiting_on` (the open decision, if there is one).
  Beside the jobs: the model server's state (reachable, loaded model, queue, as far as the
  provider says) and the number of free workers.
- **`GET /api/v1/jobs/{job}/insight`** (twin `remedy job insight <job> --json`) answers the same
  for one job, plus `stream`: the last N redacted lines of the worker's stream (N from the query,
  default 40, at most 200), each with time, role and kind (`model_text`, `tool_call` with the
  tool's name and a shortened argument, `tool_result` with its length, `system`), without secrets
  (the existing redaction); plus `advice`: a list of closed words saying what the operator can do
  (`extend_budget`, `answer_decision`, `wait`, `stop`, `apply`, `nothing`), from which a client
  makes sentences.
- Every word is in the vocabulary of `GET /api/v1/interface` (keys `now`, `insight`, `phases`,
  `stall_reasons`, `stream_kinds`, `advice`), as `story` is today.
- The data comes from what exists: `packages/orchestration/do_progress.py` (the heartbeat),
  `packages/orchestration/stream_evidence.py` (the stream), the budget actuals, the decision queue
  and the watchdog (`packages/orchestration/watchdog.py`); nothing new is recorded, only read, with
  one exception: the heartbeat also writes the time of the worker's last output into the job
  record, so `last_output_s` is right after a restart of the daemon.

## Why this exists
The operator's wish for the Orb (2026-10-20): the chat answers questions instead of asking them.
They want to know what runs now, what exactly the worker does (to look inside), what goes well,
why something hangs, and what to do when it hangs. Over HTTP Remedy lacks the data:
`GET /api/v1/jobs/{job}/story` knows five steps and `GET /api/v1/jobs/{job}/logs` the log lines;
nothing answers the current state of a running worker (task, round, last tool, seconds since the
last output, used against limit, hanging or not). In the third lab run of that day (report
`rr-20261020T143546Z`, job `48ce0648d73747eb`) the operator saw a stop and two notifications,
but not that the builder was in its twelfth model turn with 78,696 tokens spent.

Read on the code at `8ee02682d`: `do_progress.DoProgress` writes its heartbeat only to stderr of a
human-facing `remedy do` (`--json` builds none) and knows the step, not the worker's output; the
last output's time must therefore come from the stream and be persisted by the writer that
already handles it.

  `story`).

- T001, the reader. `packages/orchestration/worker_pulse.py` keeps `worker_pulse.json` in a job's
  own folder (`data_paths.worker_pulse_path`): the running call's role, provider, model, round and
  kind, the time of the worker's last output, and its last 200 stream entries, each redacted and
  cut to 200 characters. `pi_provider.bind_job_clock`, which every run of a job calls, starts a
  fresh pulse; every Pi call records its start and end, feeds each parsed stream record through
  `PiLoopWatch`'s new `on_record`, and moves the last-output time with every chunk, writing at most
