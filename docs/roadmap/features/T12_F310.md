## Goal & Done
A job's default budget comes from the profile of its provider class, its wall clock measures work
and not waiting, and a budget stop asks the operator exactly one question. DONE when:
- **Profiles per provider class.** Remedy knows a job's provider class: `local` (unpriced and on
  this machine, as `ollama`, or `pi` over a local model) or `paid` (as `anthropic`). The default
  budgets come from the class's profile, not from one number for all. Profile `local`:
  `max_total_tokens` 1,500,000, `max_provider_calls` 200, `max_wall_clock_minutes` 480,
  `min_free_disk_bytes` as today. Profile `paid`: today's `budget.*` values and the cost limit.
  The profiles are configuration (`budget.profile.local.*`, `budget.profile.paid.*`), registered
  in `docs/guides/environment.md`; an explicit value in `budget.max_*` or in the order wins as
  today. `GET /api/v1/execution` and `remedy client execution` name under `budgets` the profiles
  with their numbers and the class of every offered provider, so a client can show them as tiles.
- **The wall clock measures work.** Minutes in which the job waits for a decision, a lock or a
  model server that does not answer do not count against `max_wall_clock_minutes`; the waiting
  time is counted apart and shown in the budget actuals (`budget_actuals.waited_s`). The wall
  clock starts with the job's first model call, not with `created_at`.
- **One stop, one question.** When the budget stops a job whose contract has open blockers, no
  remainder decision is raised while the budget decision is open; the budget decision carries the
  blockers as text and offers two answers, `extend` (with limits, as today) and `abandon`. Only
  when the job stands final (after `abandon`, or at its end) and blockers stay open is the
  remainder decision raised, once. `extend` without an answer raises every limit the stop
  exhausted to twice its value (the wall clock to at least 60 minutes), so a client need not
  compute; with an answer, the answer holds. The resume after `extend` continues by itself, with
  no second call.
- **Replacing is deleting.** The places in `packages/orchestration/do_sequence.py`
  (`do_budget_stop_remainder`) and `packages/orchestration/orchestrator_loop.py` that raise the
  remainder decision on a budget stop are replaced by the new rule, not kept beside it; DECISION
  F269 D9 and F295 D11/D12 receive a dated continuation that names what was deleted.

## Why this exists
The operator ran Remedy 1.21 in the lab `~/Repos/remedy-lab` on 2026-10-20 with Pi over the local
model Muse Glimmer, where one planner call takes 40 to 76 seconds (review reports
`rr-20261020T125848Z`, `rr-20261020T130335Z`, `rr-20261020T143546Z`).
- The first order ("Bau jetzt eine minimale html showcase page nur zum testen") ran clean: one
  job, 2.5 minutes, 62,211 tokens, 7 calls, nothing applied without approval. That is the yardstick.
- The third order ("Bring die webseite auf prototyp niveau und füge funktionalität hinzu") was
  stopped after 9 minutes of wall clock in the middle of its work
  (`budget_exhausted:max_wall_clock_minutes`, job `48ce0648d73747eb`, 78,696 tokens, 3 calls, 12
  model turns of the builder). Its limits were 100,000 tokens, 5 calls and 9 minutes per job and
  85,000 tokens, 10 calls and 12 minutes per order. On a local model a 9-minute wall clock is not
  a guard against an outlier, it ends every normal piece of work. Two decisions were raised for
  the one stop (the budget question and the remainder question `td:d39ea71e`), and the Orb sent a
  notification for each (R-1370). The attempt to continue at 15:02:53 ended `stopped` again at
  once, because the time limit had not been raised.
- Read on the code at `8ee02682d`: a job's limits are the configured `budget.*` values, filled by
  the task plan's own `budgets` for every field the configuration leaves unset
  (`do_sequence.py:518`, `job_plan.apply_plan_budgets`). The lab job's 100,000 tokens exceed the
  order's 85,000 cap, so its per-job limits did not come from the order; the first round confirms
  from the job's task plan whether the 9 minutes were the local planner's own suggestion.
- The budget guard counts the wall clock from the job's start (`budget_guard.py:326` and `:417`),
  so time spent waiting for a decision or for a model server counts as work (R-1371).

The Orb cannot raise the time limit today; Universe's order of the same day (universe-clarity,
U513) repairs that, and it is not this feature's. Remedy's backend already accepts the raise
(`answer_budget_decision` with `max_wall_clock_minutes=60` answers `extended`; checked by the
operator).

## Task slicing
- T001 — The provider class and the profiles. One function names a job's provider class from its
  roles' providers and models; `budget.profile.local.*` and `budget.profile.paid.*` are read with
  the precedence explicit value > profile; `docs/guides/environment.md` registers every key; the
  execution offer names the profiles and each offered provider's class.
- T002 — The working wall clock. The guard reads the wall clock from the first model call and
  subtracts the waited seconds; the waits (decision, lock, unreachable model server) are recorded
  where they already happen and summed into `budget_actuals.waited_s`.
- T003 — One stop, one question. The budget decision carries the open blockers; `extend` without
  an answer doubles the exhausted limits (wall clock at least 60); the resume follows by itself;
  the remainder decision is raised only for a final job; the two old raise sites are deleted.
- T004 — The proof. The tests named under Acceptance, the lab stop of 2026-10-20 rebuilt from its
  report as a fixture, and `docs/system/job-budget-enforcement-v0.md` updated.

## Acceptance
- A job with a local provider and no explicit budget runs with the local profile; an explicit
  `budget.max_*` value or an order cap still wins.
- The lab stop of 2026-10-20, rebuilt from report `rr-20261020T143546Z`, raises exactly one
  decision; after `abandon` with blockers open, the remainder decision is raised once.
- `extend` without an answer doubles the exhausted limits and the job continues without a second
  call.
- Waiting for a decision does not lengthen the wall clock; `budget_actuals.waited_s` shows it.
- The existing budget tests stay green; R-1370 and R-1371 carry resolution lines naming this
  feature's evidence.
