# T3_F116 — Cost anomaly alarm
**Tier 3 · Depends on: F103 · Enhanced by: F074 class expectations (config defaults until then) · Blocks/used by: protection in unattended operation**

## Goal & Done
A runaway between checkpoints can no longer burn quietly: a burn-rate
detector watches spend velocity per job against expectations, and an
outlier raises a warning event and — on unattended runs — a pause
decision; optionally it throttles parallel width. DONE when a fixture
burn spike trips the detector with the numbers in the decision, normal
expensive-but-expected work does not trip it, and the detector shares
its definition of "expected" with the watchdog story (one home per
rule).

## How it fits (inspect current shape before building)
The ledger provides timestamped per-call costs — the detector is a
sliding-window query, evaluated at the same cadence as other run-level
checks (call finalization / batch boundaries), not a background thread.
The autonomy watchdog already owns a SELF-RELATIVE burn tripwire for
missions; this feature owns the CLASS-EXPECTATION check for jobs and,
per that feature's note, UNIFIES the two: the watchdog's burn tripwire
becomes a consumer of this detector — remove the duplicate there as
part of this feature (one detector, two callers). Notifications attach
when the notification feature lands; until then surfacing is decision +
status + report.

## Design (suggested shape)
- `burn_detector.py`: evaluate(job|mission, window) → {rate_usd_h,
  expected_basis (class_default|trailing_baseline), multiplier,
  tripped} — expectation = class expectation where bands exist, else
  the run's own trailing baseline; trip when rate exceeds expectation
  by a config multiplier with a minimum-spend floor (tiny absolute
  numbers never trip — noise discipline).
- On trip (attended): a prominent warning in status/report + a ledger
  event. On trip (unattended/--yes): additionally a pause decision with
  the arithmetic (window, rate, expectation, basis) and options
  resume|abandon; optional parallel-width throttle to a config floor
  while the decision is open.
- Dedup: one open trip decision per run; re-trips update its evidence.
- Unification: replace the watchdog's internal burn check with a call
  into this detector (its tests keep passing — the behavior contract
  stays).

## Task slicing
- **T001** detector + window math + floors/multipliers + table tests
  (spike trips; expected-expensive doesn't; tiny-spend never).
- **T002** trip actions per mode + dedup + throttle hook + fixture
  runs (attended warn; unattended pause with arithmetic).
- **T003** watchdog unification (one detector) + its regression suite
  green + docs.

## Slice order (written at the claim, 2026-10-07)
DECISION F116 D1 fixes it. T001 first, because there is nothing to call or to unify into until the
detector exists: a pure function over spend samples that holds the one definition of "expected",
with its basis labels, floors and multiplier, proved by table tests. T002 then calls it at the job
runner's safe point and acts on a trip, a warning when attended and a pause decision with the
arithmetic when unattended, after first deciding which job runs count as unattended and where the
samples come from. T003 makes the watchdog's burn tripwire a caller of the detector and deletes the
watchdog's own arithmetic, with its tests still green and the docs updated. Then the hardening
stage of operator amendment amend0930b-slow-cap, then the closure sequence.

## Acceptance
- Spike fixture trips with correct arithmetic in the decision; the
  steady-expensive fixture doesn't. Unattended trip pauses before the
  next dispatch. Watchdog burn tests pass against the unified
  detector. All rates/expectations carry basis labels.

## Edge cases & assumption defaults (A9)
- Bursty-but-legit patterns (many tiny calls at batch start): window
  minimum spans a full batch — documented. Clock skew: all timestamps
  UTC from the ledger; no wall-clock reads in the math.

## Orchestrator brief
Gate on the ledger and the watchdog. T003's unification is the
architectural point — require the watchdog diff to DELETE its private
check, not wrap it.

## Do not touch
Notification channels, budget limits (separate mechanism), calibration.
Suggested tests: tests/orchestration/test_burn_detector.py.

## Built State (F116, 2026-10-07)

What exists on disk before the closure sequence of F116, built on
`feature/f116-cost-anomaly-alarm`, cut from `main` at `e80b95467`. Every DECISION named lives in
`.agent/decisions.md`; every round's verdict in `.agent/live_review.md` or, once rotated, its
archive.

**T001 — the burn detector.** `packages/orchestration/burn_detector.py` holds the one definition
of expected spend, `evaluate_burn_rate`, a pure function over spend samples that reads no clock,
no file and no configuration (DECISION F116 D2). Its reading carries the rate, the expectation,
the basis label — `trailing_baseline` per sample, or `class_default` per hour when a configured
hourly expectation exists — the multiplier, the window and whether it tripped; it trips when the
window spent more than a floor and its rate is strictly above the multiplier times the
expectation. `tests/orchestration/test_burn_detector.py` holds the spike, steady-expensive,
tiny-spend, boundary and agreement tables.

**T002 — the job runner acts on a trip.** `packages/orchestration/job_burn.py` measures a job in
tokens per counted provider call, configured by five `job_burn.*` settings (D3). `run_job` reads
it at every safe point where no stop fired and keeps the newest trip on the job as `burn_reading`
(D4). A job whose plan was approved unattended, as `remedy do run --yes` records, is paused before
its next provider call and gets one `[burn_alarm]` decision with the arithmetic in a sentence, the
options `resume` and `abandon` and no safe default; a re-trip updates it, and a failed pause
request blocks the job (D5, D6 (1)). Its impact names `remedy job run <job_id>` as what continues
the job, because the park consumes the pause request, and answering the decision only records the
choice (D9 (1)). A job a person started keeps running. No parallel-width throttle is built,
because a job runs one task and one provider call at a time (D7 (4)).

**T003 — one detector, two callers, and what a person sees.** The watchdog's
`evaluate_burn_anomaly` in `packages/orchestration/watchdog.py` now only turns ledger entries into
samples and the reading into its trip; its own arithmetic is deleted, its trip carries `basis`,
and a negative token total no longer counts (D6). `remedy job show` prints a "Burn alarm" block,
the report section of `remedy job show --full` carries `burn_alarm`, `remedy job budget` prints
the recorded alarm whatever limits the job has, none included (D7, D9 (2)), and a new trip writes
one `job_burn_tripped` run-log event (D8). `docs/system/cost-anomaly-alarm-v1.md` documents the
whole alarm.

**The hardening stage.** Because the sessions ran in SLOW MODE, a fresh auditor who saw only this
file and the repository split the statements under Goal & Done and Acceptance into 24 claims and
tried to break each one with a mutation of the production code (operator amendment
amend0930b-slow-cap, `.agent/f116_acceptance_audit.md`). 21 had a test that failed under its
mutation at once, four of them also through the command line, and one, the optional throttle, is
not built by design. It found two gaps: no test checked the first trip's decision text whole
(R-1173), and `remedy job budget` hid the alarm from a job with no budgets (R-1174). The reviewer
added a third from the auditor's notes: the decision named `remedy job unpause` where
`remedy job run` continues the job (R-1175). One repair round closed all three, and a repeat audit
(`.agent/f116_acceptance_reaudit1.md`) proved each closed, through the command line as well. No
gap remains open. The repeat audit also noted that no test reads the sentence of
`docs/system/cost-anomaly-alarm-v1.md` that names the continuing command; that sentence is
documentation rather than a statement of this file, and the reviewer read it true.

**Reachability (closure precondition 7).** This feature added two modules,
`packages/orchestration/burn_detector.py` and `packages/orchestration/job_burn.py`; production
code imports both, and the two lines this feature adds to
`tests/orchestration/import_reachability_allowlist.txt` list them. It adds nothing to
`ALLOWED_UNWIRED` or `RESERVED_NAMESPACES`: the entries its building rounds placed in
`ALLOWED_UNWIRED` were deleted once each module gained its importer.
